The problem

8,000,000+

Kenyans are blacklisted by credit bureaus — most over mobile loans, many under KES 200.

A credit listing locks people out of the formal economy: jobs, rent, further credit. Borrowers can't see a loan's true cost, don't know whether a lender is even licensed, and don't know the rights they already have.

Then comes harassment, contact-scraping, and repossession — household distress at scale. The rights and public facts exist. They're just invisible to the people who need them.

Why the harm is invisible

Two facts are hidden at the moment you decide.

The cost

"Borrow 1,000, repay 1,150 in 30 days" looks like 15%. It is 182.5% APR. A phone at "KES 60/day" is KES 23,900 for a KES 12,000 phone — 99% more.

The right

Is the lender licensed by CBK? Can they legally chase you? What can they lawfully take if you miss a payment? Almost no borrower knows to ask.
